# ClickAway: stress-test four novel hooks

You are one of three game-design judges (Sol, Terra and Sonnet). The owner is pivoting ClickAway, a browser reflex game where you click shrinking targets for combos, into an incremental game with a wide audience. **Do not edit, create or delete any files in this repo.**

Background, if useful: `docs/concepts/concept-brief.md` describes today's game; `docs/concepts/app-tour/` has screenshots; `docs/concepts/reference-favorites.png` is the owner's list of favourite incrementals.

## What the owner wants
The owner rejected remaking existing games (a factory idle, a Cauldron-like arcade, a Digseum-like dig). They want a **novel idea**. Their model is Scritchy Scratchy, which is popular because it is **easily relatable and addictive**. Our reading of why that works:
1. **It copies a real-world micro-ritual almost everyone has done** (scratching a lottery ticket), so it needs no tutorial.
2. **Every action is a small reveal with a variable reward**, the "what's under there?" pull.
3. **Upgrades transform the ritual itself**: bigger cards, faster scratching, then automatic scratchers. The same act grows into an empire.

## The four candidate hooks
**1. Spare Change: dig loose coins out of couch cushions, then out of the world.**
- **The click:** glints in the cushions are the targets, and they shrink and hide as you dig deeper. Most are pennies; rare finds are a lost earring, a gift card or a ring.
- **The climb:** couch, car seats, laundromat, arcade coin returns, mall fountain, wishing wells, a metal-detector beach, sunken treasure.
- **Automation:** a raccoon, then a vacuum with a coin filter, then neighbourhood kids paid in candy.
- **Prestige ("spend it all"):** blow the fortune on something absurd, keep a collection of rare finds, and start again in a new city.

**2. Fortune Cookie Factory: every reward is a fortune that changes the rules.**
- **The click:** crack cookies. The fortune inside is the upgrade ("Your next 10 cookies will be lucky", "A stranger brings you double", "Avoid the left side of the tray today").
- **Golden fortunes** permanently rewrite how the factory works. Fortunes are funny, quotable and screenshot-able.
- **Progression:** a corner bakery grows into a cookie empire. Prestige is a new zodiac year, and each animal brings its own fortune set.

**3. Bubble Wrap Shipping Co.: pop the wrap, open the package.**
- **The click:** pop bubbles (some hide stickers or coins), then unbox a mystery item to sell or keep.
- **Progression:** apartment, mail room, warehouse, global hub.
- **Upgrades:** a rolling pin that pops whole rows, packing peanuts that hide extras, fragile boxes worth more if you pop without breaking them.

**4. Geode Cracker: tap rocks open for crystals.**
- **The click:** hits crack the rock along weak points (the targets). A clean break gives a better crystal.
- **Progression:** polish and display finds in a museum. Prestige moves to a new quarry with different rock types.

## Your job
Stress-test **every** hook, then make the case for each one as if you had to champion it, and say what you would do differently. Use web research to check **novelty**: find any existing games with the same hook (Steam, itch.io, mobile stores, Newgrounds, Kongregate), with links and, where available, review counts. A hook that has already been done well is a weaker bet unless your version has a clear twist.

For each hook, give:
- **Relatable?** Does almost everyone recognise the ritual instantly? Score 1–5.
- **Addictive?** Is there a reveal with a variable reward on every click, and does it still pull at hour 10? Score 1–5.
- **Novel?** What already exists, with links, and how crowded is it? Score 1–5.
- **Scales?** Does the ritual transform through upgrades into something bigger, and does prestige change decisions? Score 1–5.
- **Fits ClickAway?** Does the existing target / combo / powerup / build tech fit naturally? Score 1–5.
- **The case for it**: its best pitch in two or three sentences, the way you'd sell it to a player.
- **What I'd do differently**: concrete changes to the hook, the twist that makes it stand out, the first upgrades, and the prestige. Rename it if a better name exists.
- **Biggest risk.**

Then:
- **YOUR RANKING** of all four, with one line of reasoning each.
- **A FIFTH HOOK (optional):** if you know a relatable everyday ritual that beats all four on these criteria, pitch it briefly in the same format.
- **YOUR PICK**: the one you would build, and the smallest playable version that proves the addiction in under 15 minutes of play.

About 1,400 words. Be concrete: example finds, numbers, upgrade names and real competitor links beat adjectives.
